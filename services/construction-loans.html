How construction financing works

A construction mortgage isn’t advanced as one lump sum. Funds are released in progress draws tied to completion stages, verified by an appraiser:

  • Land / foundation draw — typically at ~15–20% completion
  • Lock-up draw — framing, roof, windows and doors (~40%)
  • Drywall draw — interior systems complete (~65%)
  • Completion draw — occupancy-ready (~97–100%)

During construction you typically pay interest only on funds drawn. At completion, the loan converts to (or is replaced by) a standard mortgage.

Where I add value

Frequently asked questions

How much down payment does a construction loan need?
Plan for 25–35% of the total project cost (land + build) with most lenders. Existing land equity counts toward it.
Can I be my own general contractor?
Some lenders allow self-builds with a strong plan and experience; others require a licensed builder. I know which is which and will match you accordingly.
What happens when construction finishes?
The loan converts to a standard mortgage (or we refinance to the best market rate at that moment). I set up the end financing from day one so there are no surprises.
Does it cover major renovations too?
Yes — purchase-plus-improvements and renovation draws work on the same principle and can fund large remodels or additions.
